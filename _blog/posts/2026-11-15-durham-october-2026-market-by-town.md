The Toronto Regional Real Estate Board (TRREB) released its October 2026 figures on November 4. Across the Greater Toronto Area the story was a cautious one: TRREB reported 5,603 home sales, down 7.9 per cent from October 2025, and 14,173 new listings, down 11.9 per cent. The board pointed to uncertainty around inflation, borrowing costs and the wider economy as the reason many buyers stayed on the sidelines.

The regional headline hides a lot of local detail. Here is what the same report shows for Durham Region, and why it matters for anyone getting a home ready for market this season.

## Durham at a glance

For October 2026, TRREB's Market Watch reports these figures for Durham Region across all home types:

- **Sales:** 589
- **New listings:** 1,700
- **Active listings at month end:** 2,619
- **Average price:** $793,024
- **Average sale-to-list price ratio:** 98 per cent
- **Average days on market:** 29

Two of those numbers are worth reading together. Durham recorded 1,700 new listings against 589 sales, which works out to just under three new listings for every home that sold in the month. That is our arithmetic from TRREB's figures, not a TRREB statistic, but it describes the situation plainly: buyers had choice, and most homes sold slightly under their asking price.

## Town by town

The Market Watch breaks Durham down by municipality. Here are October's sales and new listings for each:

- **Oshawa:** 152 sales, 396 new listings
- **Clarington:** 111 sales, 314 new listings
- **Whitby:** 108 sales, 325 new listings
- **Pickering:** 93 sales, 285 new listings
- **Ajax:** 74 sales, 200 new listings
- **Scugog:** 27 sales, 74 new listings
- **Brock:** 13 sales, 47 new listings
- **Uxbridge:** 11 sales, 59 new listings

Oshawa was the busiest market in the region on both counts. Whitby, Clarington and Pickering each added roughly 285 to 325 new listings in the month. In the smaller northern townships the numbers are small enough that a handful of listings can change the picture from one month to the next, so they are best read over several months rather than one.

## What a market with choice asks of a listing

When buyers have several comparable homes to look at, the first decision they make is which ones are worth a visit. That decision usually happens on a phone, scrolling through listing photos, long before anyone books a showing.

A few practical points follow from that, whatever the price point:

- **The first image carries the listing.** In a busy feed, the lead photo decides whether a buyer taps in. It should show the home's strongest feature in good light, not just the front door.
- **Light and order matter more than gear.** Lights on, blinds open, counters cleared and cars off the driveway make a bigger difference to a photo than any lens.
- **Show the setting, not just the rooms.** In towns like Scugog, Uxbridge and Clarington, lot size, privacy and the surrounding land are often what a buyer is paying for. Aerial images, where they can be flown safely and legally, show that in one frame.
- **Floor plans answer the question photos can't.** A simple 2D plan tells a buyer how rooms connect, which helps the right buyers book and the wrong ones move on.
- **Be accurate.** Edits should make a home look its best, not different. Removing a temporary eyesore is one thing; changing what a buyer will actually find is another, and it costs trust at the showing.

## The months ahead

TRREB noted that, on a seasonally adjusted basis, October sales and new listings were both up from September, and its president said a Canada-U.S. trade deal could give GTA residents a clearer picture of the economic outlook. None of that guarantees a turn, but it does mean sellers listing through late fall and winter are competing for the attention of buyers who are watching closely and comparing carefully.

That is exactly when presentation earns its keep. A listing that looks its best on the first scroll gives a home its fair chance against the two or three others on the same street.
