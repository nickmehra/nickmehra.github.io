We have looked at the Toronto and Durham numbers for October on this journal already. This week the national picture is worth a look too, because it shows the same pattern from a wider angle: buyers are not gone, but they have more to choose from and less pressure to decide quickly.

The Canadian Real Estate Association (CREA) published its October 2026 figures on November 17. Here is what they say, and what they suggest for anyone listing a home this winter.

## The headline numbers

According to CREA's release:

- **Sales edged up from September.** National home sales rose 0.3% month over month on a seasonally adjusted basis.
- **But they were well below last year.** Actual, not seasonally adjusted, sales were down 10.6% from October 2025. The Canadian Press reported 37,827 sales for the month.
- **Prices were roughly flat.** The seasonally adjusted MLS Home Price Index rose 0.1% from September. On a non-adjusted basis it was down 3.1% from a year earlier, a smaller decline than in September.
- **The average sale price** was $678,584, down 1.6% from a year earlier. CREA notes that this figure is not seasonally adjusted.
- **Supply is healthy.** About 197,000 properties were listed for sale at the end of October, a little above the long-term average of roughly 190,000. That works out to five months of inventory nationally, in line with the long-term average.
- **The sales-to-new listings ratio** was 48.7%, compared with a long-term average of 54.7%.

CREA's next monthly release is scheduled for December 15.

## Where Ontario fits

CREA's release does not break the numbers down by province. Ratehub.ca, reporting on the same data, said Ontario sales rose 1.7% from September on a seasonally adjusted basis, and that national sales would have slipped slightly without Ontario's gain. Ratehub also reported that Ontario sales for the first ten months of the year were down 3.8% from the same period in 2025.

For local detail, the TRREB figures we covered earlier this month remain the better guide for the GTA and Durham. The national numbers simply confirm the direction.

## What a steady, well-supplied market means for listing media

When prices are flat and buyers have about five months of choice, a listing is competing on presentation as much as on price. A few practical points follow from that:

- **Buyers compare more.** With more homes to look at, people scroll further and shortlist harder. The first photo and the first few seconds of a video decide whether a listing makes the shortlist at all.
- **Accuracy builds trust.** In a market where buyers are not rushed, they look closely. Galleries that show the home honestly, with clear room-to-room flow, help serious buyers decide to book a showing instead of moving on.
- **Winter light needs planning.** Shorter days mean the shoot has to be scheduled around the light, a subject we covered in an earlier post on winter listings.
- **Fewer, better assets.** A clean, consistent photo set, a short walkthrough, and aerials where they genuinely add context usually do more than a long gallery of similar angles.

## Reading the numbers carefully

Two cautions are worth keeping in mind. First, month-over-month changes are seasonally adjusted, while year-over-year comparisons use actual figures, so the two are not directly comparable. Second, the average price is influenced by the mix of homes that happen to sell in a given month.

None of this tells an individual seller what their home is worth. That is a conversation for their agent, using local comparables. What it does show is a market where a well-presented listing has room to stand out.
