Ask three sources how big a house is and you can get three different answers. The assessment roll says one thing, the builder's brochure says another, and a floor plan drawn from a scan says a third. None of them is necessarily wrong. They are just measuring different things.

That gap is where listing problems start. Square footage is a figure buyers use to compare homes, and in Ontario it is held to the same accuracy standard as everything else an agent puts in front of the public.

## What the rules actually say

Neither RECO's advertising bulletin nor the Code of Ethics sets out a method for measuring a resale home. What applies instead are the general duties in the Code of Ethics under the *Trust in Real Estate Services Act, 2002*. Section 5 says a registrant must make best efforts to make sure any representation is accurate and not misleading, and must not take part in misrepresentation. Section 9 requires reasonable knowledge, skill, judgment and competence when giving information to anyone.

RECO's advertising bulletin adds that advertising has to be factually correct, accurate and verifiable. It also makes a point that fits square footage well: a statement can be technically true and still inaccurate if it is vague or incomplete where clarity matters. The bulletin does not mention floor area specifically, but a size figure in a listing is a representation like any other.

## Where size figures come from

Most of the confusion comes from treating different measurements as if they were the same.

**MPAC.** The Municipal Property Assessment Corporation measures the outside of a home to estimate its living area. It leaves out basements, decks, porches and garages, counts open space to the second floor as second-floor area, and includes third and upper floors. Its data comes from building permits, architectural plans, digital imagery and inspections, and where an inspector cannot get inside, MPAC may estimate interior features from the exterior. Assessments also reflect the property as of the January 1, 2016 valuation date. A finished basement or a recent addition can make a big difference between MPAC's figure and what a buyer walks through.

**Builder plans.** For new homes, the Home Construction Regulatory Authority (HCRA) sets a standard method for licensees that state floor area in advertising or sales material. Above-grade floors are measured to the outside face of exterior walls, finished basements are listed separately, and garages and unfinished space are excluded. The directive allows a tolerance of 2 per cent either way and requires a note that actual usable space may vary. It applies to HCRA licensees marketing new homes, not to resale listings, but it explains why a brochure figure from a decade ago may not match a later measurement.

**On-site measurement.** A floor plan drawn from a laser or scan measurement can show interior space room by room. Depending on how its totals are calculated, it may or may not include the basement, stairs or walls. That is often the most accurate picture of usable space, but it is a different number from MPAC's exterior estimate or a builder's gross floor area.

## How a floor plan should present size

A floor plan is part of the listing, so it should help buyers understand the number rather than add a fourth one with no context.

- **Say what was measured.** Label totals as interior or exterior, and show above-grade and basement areas separately rather than blending them.
- **Name the source.** If the listing figure comes from MPAC, the builder or the floor plan, say so. A short line such as "interior area per floor plan" removes most of the guesswork.
- **Mark approximate figures as approximate.** Plan dimensions are usually rounded, and walls are rarely perfectly square. A clear note that measurements are approximate is appropriate, but it should sit alongside a figure you have reason to believe, not replace checking it.
- **Keep everything consistent.** The square footage in the MLS remarks, the feature sheet, the floor plan and the video caption should match, or the difference should be explained. Mismatched numbers are what buyers notice after closing.
- **Watch for big gaps.** If the floor plan total and MPAC differ widely, find out why before the listing goes live. Often it is a finished basement or an addition, and saying so up front is easier than explaining it later.

> The best square footage figure is one you can explain: where it came from, what it includes and why it may differ from the others.
