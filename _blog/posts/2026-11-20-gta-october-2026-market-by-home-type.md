Last week we looked at Durham Region's October numbers town by town. This time we are stepping back to the whole Greater Toronto Area and splitting the market by the kind of home being sold. The Toronto Regional Real Estate Board's October 2026 Market Watch shows that detached houses, semis, townhouses and condo apartments are each in a slightly different market, and that should shape how each is marketed.

## The GTA overall

Across all TRREB areas, October 2026 brought 5,603 sales, down 7.9 per cent from October 2025, and 14,173 new listings, down 11.9 per cent. The average selling price was $1,024,108, down 2.7 per cent, and the MLS Home Price Index composite benchmark stood at $916,400, down 4.3 per cent year over year.

Active listings at month end totalled 25,098. Homes sold on average for 97 per cent of their asking price, after an average of 31 days on market. TRREB's trend figures, which are based on a 12-month moving average, put the sales-to-new-listings ratio at 37.9 per cent and months of inventory at 4.6. In plain terms, buyers have options and time to compare them.

## Detached houses

Detached homes were the largest segment, with 2,693 sales, down 4.4 per cent from a year earlier. The average price was $1,310,627, down 3.2 per cent, and the benchmark price for a single-family detached home was $1,194,200, down 4.4 per cent.

There were 6,687 new detached listings in the month. Detached homes sold at 97 per cent of asking on average, after 29 days on market.

## Semi-detached houses

Semis were the tightest part of the market in October. TRREB recorded 546 sales, down 8.1 per cent, at an average price of $1,024,907, almost flat at minus 0.8 per cent. The single-family attached benchmark was $914,300, down 3.8 per cent.

Semis drew 1,084 new listings, sold on average at 101 per cent of asking and took 22 days to sell, the fastest of the four home types.

## Townhouses

TRREB's summary counts 933 townhouse sales, down 11.0 per cent, at an average of $804,416, down 5.0 per cent. The townhouse benchmark price was $662,200, down 6.6 per cent.

The detail pages split this group in two, and the halves behave differently. Freehold attached, row and townhouses had 508 sales against 1,096 new listings, sold at 99 per cent of asking and took 28 days. Condo townhouses had 425 sales against 1,062 new listings, sold at 97 per cent of asking and took 38 days.

## Condo apartments

Condo apartments had 1,385 sales, down 10.4 per cent, at an average price of $621,400, down 5.8 per cent. The apartment benchmark was $521,600, down 7.3 per cent, the steepest annual decline of any home type in the index.

New condo apartment listings reached 4,088, with 8,100 active at month end. Units sold at 97 per cent of asking after an average of 38 days.

## Comparing the segments

Putting sales beside new listings shows where competition is highest. By our own arithmetic from TRREB's figures (not a TRREB statistic), October had roughly one sale for every two new semi listings, a little under one for every two freehold townhouse listings, about two in five for detached houses and condo townhouses, and about one in three for condo apartments.

## The national picture

The Canadian Real Estate Association's October release, published November 17, shows the same mood across the country. National sales were down 10.6 per cent from October 2025 on an actual basis, but edged up 0.3 per cent from September on a seasonally adjusted basis. CREA put the national sales-to-new-listings ratio at 48.7 per cent and inventory at five months, close to the long-term average. The national average price was $678,584, down 1.6 per cent.

## What this means for presentation

The figures point to a simple rule: the more listings a buyer can choose from in your segment, the harder your photos and video need to work.

- **Condo apartments: earn the click.** With the most listings per sale and the longest average time on market, a condo has to stand out among many similar units. Lead with the view or the best-lit living space, add a floor plan, and give amenities a few clean frames. A short walkthrough video helps a buyer understand flow before visiting.
- **Condo townhouses: show what sets the unit apart.** They took as long to sell as apartments. Private outdoor space, end-unit light or a finished basement are worth leading with, along with the setting of the complex.
- **Detached houses: sell the property, not just the rooms.** At these prices buyers are weighing lot, street and neighbourhood. Aerial photos, flown safely and within the rules, show yard size, privacy and proximity to parks or schools in a single frame. Twilight exteriors give a listing a distinct thumbnail in a crowded feed.
- **Semis and freehold townhouses: still do it properly.** These moved fastest and closest to asking, which means the first weekend matters most. Have strong photos and a floor plan live on day one so the listing captures its share of early showings rather than catching up later.

Whatever the home type, the basics hold: good light, tidy rooms, honest edits and a lead image that shows the home's best feature.

## Looking ahead

TRREB noted that seasonally adjusted sales and new listings both rose from September. That does not guarantee a stronger winter, but buyers are active, careful and comparing, and presentation is one of the few things a seller fully controls.
