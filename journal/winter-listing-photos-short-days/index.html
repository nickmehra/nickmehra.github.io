Listing in winter: how to plan photos around Durham's short days

In Durham, the sun sets before 4:50 p.m. from mid-November through the solstice. Here is how to schedule a winter listing shoot around the light, and why the quieter December market makes good photos count.

Most listing advice assumes a long summer evening. From now until the new year, Durham doesn't get one. If you're listing a home between late November and the holidays, the light is the tightest constraint on the shoot, and planning around it is most of what separates a gallery that looks warm and bright from one that looks grey and rushed.

How much daylight there actually is

Using Oshawa as a reference point for Durham, published sunrise and sunset tables for 2026 show:

  • November 17: sunrise at 7:10 a.m., sunset at 4:49 p.m., about 9 hours 39 minutes of daylight.
  • December 1: sunrise at 7:27 a.m., sunset at 4:41 p.m., about 9 hours 13 minutes.
  • December 9: the earliest sunset of the year, at 4:39 p.m.
  • December 21: sunrise at 7:44 a.m., sunset at 4:42 p.m., just under 9 hours of daylight.

The same tables put dusk (the end of usable twilight) roughly half an hour after sunset, around 5:15 p.m. for most of December. That half hour is short, and it arrives before most people finish work.

Notice the odd detail in those numbers: sunset is earliest in early December, but mornings keep getting later through the solstice. For scheduling, that means afternoons stop shrinking before mornings do.

Schedule the shoot around the house, not the calendar

In summer, a photographer can usually fit a shoot in whenever the seller is ready. In December, the order of the day matters.

  • Know which way the house faces. A south-facing front gets the most direct light in winter, because the sun stays low in the southern sky all day. A north-facing front can look flat and blue in midday shade. Ask your photographer to check orientation before booking a time slot.
  • Book interiors for the brightest window of the day. Late morning to early afternoon gives rooms the most natural light. Starting a full interior shoot at 3 p.m. in December leaves little room for error.
  • Leave the twilight exterior for last. If you want a dusk shot with the lights on, plan to have interiors finished, lights checked and the photographer outside by about 4:30 p.m. The window closes quickly.
  • Plan drone time early. Aerial work needs daylight and a little extra time for setup, battery changes in the cold and checks before flight. Putting it mid-day keeps it from competing with the twilight shot.

Prepare the property for snow and short light

Winter shoots go better when the seller has a short checklist the day before:

  • Clear the driveway, walkways and steps fully, and pull bins and shovels out of frame. A half-cleared path reads as neglect in a photo.
  • Turn on every light, and replace burnt-out bulbs, including exterior fixtures, porch lights and under-cabinet lighting. Mismatched bulb colours show up more in winter photos because rooms rely on them more.
  • Open every blind and curtain to use whatever daylight there is.
  • Put holiday decorations away or keep them minimal. A gallery with a tree and wreaths dates the listing the moment January arrives, and it can distract from the rooms themselves.
  • Keep fresh snow untouched on the lawn if you can. Clean, even snow photographs well; tracked, patchy snow doesn't.

If the home has a pool, garden or deck that sells it in summer, ask whether earlier photos exist. A clearly labelled seasonal photo can help buyers picture the property, as long as it's accurate and current enough that nothing material has changed.

Why it's worth the effort in a quiet month

New listings thin out sharply at the end of the year. In TRREB's December 2025 report, the Greater Toronto Area recorded 5,299 new listings for the month, compared with 186,753 new listings across all of 2025. Sales in December 2025 were 3,697, down 8.9 per cent from the year before.

That means buyers who are searching in December are scrolling through far fewer fresh listings. A listing that looks bright and well cared for in the middle of a grey week has less new competition for their attention.

That cuts both ways. A dark, cramped-looking gallery has fewer listings to hide behind, too.

What to ask your photographer

Before you book a winter listing shoot, a few questions settle most of the planning:

  • What time will you arrive, and what order will you shoot in?
  • Do you want me to schedule a twilight exterior, and what time do you need the lights on?
  • Will drone footage happen on the same visit, and what happens if the weather turns?
  • Can we move the date if there's a heavy snowfall or freezing rain the day before?

Good answers will mention the sunset time without being prompted.
