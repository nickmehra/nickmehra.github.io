Drone shots for your listing: what Canada's rules actually require

Aerial photos sell the lot, the street and the setting. Here is what Transport Canada requires before a drone goes up over your listing, and the questions to ask whoever flies it.

A front-door photo shows a house. An aerial shows the lot line, the tree cover, the distance to the water and how the driveway fits two cars and a trailer. For acreage, estates and anything with a view, the drone often carries the listing.

It is also the part of a shoot that is regulated by federal law. If you are hiring someone to fly over a property you represent, it is worth knowing what they are supposed to have.

Weight decides the paperwork

Transport Canada draws its first line at 250 grams. A drone under that is a microdrone. At 250 grams or more, the drone has to be registered and the pilot has to hold a pilot certificate, either for Basic Operations or Advanced Operations. Registration costs $10 as of April 1, 2025, and the registration number has to be marked on the aircraft.

Most professional camera drones are well over 250 grams, so for listing work you should expect both a registered drone and a certified pilot.

Basic or Advanced: it comes down to where and how close

The category depends on the flight, not the person's résumé:

  • Basic operations stay in uncontrolled airspace, more than 30 metres (100 feet) horizontally from bystanders, and never over them.
  • Advanced operations cover flying in controlled airspace, within 30 metres of bystanders, or over them. These need the Advanced certificate, which includes an in-person flight review.

Either way, the drone stays below 122 metres (400 feet) and within the pilot's sight. Flights beyond that need newer certificates and conditions that rarely apply to a residential listing.

In practice, parts of Durham and the GTA sit inside controlled airspace around airports and heliports. Flying there needs an Advanced certificate and permission from NAV CANADA, so the address should be checked before the shoot is booked, not on the driveway. A tight suburban street with neighbours out front can also push a flight into Advanced territory because of the 30-metre rule.

What changed in 2025

Transport Canada rolled out the biggest update to the drone rules in years in two phases, on April 1 and November 4, 2025. Two changes are worth knowing for real estate:

  • Sheltered operations. Since November 4, 2025, Advanced pilots can fly close to a building without a visual observer, provided they stay within 61 metres horizontally of the structure, no more than 30 metres above it, and more than 30 metres from uninvolved people. That suits detailed roof and exterior work.
  • Microdrones at advertised events. Since April 1, 2025, even a sub-250-gram drone needs a Special Flight Operations Certificate to fly at an advertised outdoor event such as a market, festival or open-air launch. A public "grand opening" with a drone flyover is not a free-for-all.

Five questions to ask before anyone flies your listing

  • Is the drone registered, and what certificate do you hold, Basic or Advanced?
  • Is this address in controlled airspace, and if so, how are you getting NAV CANADA permission?
  • How will you handle neighbours, foot traffic and anyone in the yard on the day?
  • Do you carry liability insurance that covers drone work?
  • What do I get, and when: stills, video, and in what turnaround?

A good operator will answer all five without hesitation. It is part of the job.

The aerial should make the property look its best without anyone having to wonder whether the shot was legal.
